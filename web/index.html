The runner-up

A neural network of 432 numbers was trained to do one small thing: read ten numbers, one at a time, and point to the second-largest.

This page takes it apart until you can see what each of its sixteen neurons does, how they decide together, and why they sometimes get it wrong.

Every figure is computed from the network's 432 numbers, the painting included: the ink is its links, the circles its neurons.

The whole machine, in four sentences

A record keeper and four comparators measure how far each new number lands below the record. Their memory weights turn the memory like the hand of a clock, so how far it has turned says when the record and the runner-up arrived. Ten timers read that clock and vote, each one for its own positions, and the votes add up exactly to the scores. The mistakes sit where two numbers nearly tie, where no smooth machine can be sure, and in one corner where a shortcut the network learned points confidently the wrong way.

To poke at it yourself, the explorer lets you click any neuron or weight, edit any input, and watch the chain reaction behind any answer.
