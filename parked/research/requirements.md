# Why structure loses on accuracy

> Why no estimate built from the model's structure beats sampling on accuracy, the common-event control, each constraint pinned to a number.
> Nothing tried meets them, and the stratified lattice holds the event without reading the model, so accuracy is a control and rare events are the yardstick ([benchmarks.md](../../benchmarks.md)). The one asymmetry found, that exactness is cheap at the end of the sequence, is worth the size of a lattice shift.

## The accounting
Sampling's MSE at C ops is p(1 − p) · 2880 / C. The family-stratified lattice sits at 0.35 of it from 1e8 ops up, and that constant is the share of the indicator's variance inside a (family, T, M) cell, 30–41% ([sharing_limits](../results/experiments/sharing_limits.txt)): stratification removes the between-cell variance for free, and the within-cell part, which S and the seven lower inputs decide through gate flips, converges at the sampling rate, slope −0.6 ([family-lattice](angles/family-lattice.md)). So a winning estimator must either remove part of that residual by deduction at under one pass per node, or converge faster than N^−0.6 on it.

## Six constraints
| A solution must | Because | Number |
|---|---|---|
| Deduce more than the last step's kinks | Exact integration is cheap only at the end of the sequence: the last input's sweep costs 0.27 passes for 45% of the variance, the S-line 4–7 passes for 50–75%, and two directions jointly need polygon measures, pieces^d beyond | [cheap_suffix](../results/experiments/cheap_suffix.txt), [sline_structure](../../results/experiments/sline_structure.txt) |
| Not share prefixes | One shared step saves a tenth of a pass, and leaves under one prefix are correlated: trees over the last one or two inputs score 0.34–0.47 against the lattice's 0.36–0.39 | [cheap_suffix](../results/experiments/cheap_suffix.txt) |
| Hold a presumption over the lower inputs to 1e-4 at under a pass | The Gaussian margin errs 7e-4 with oracle moments and 1.2e-3 with the 16-run design; one pass misses 10–40% of the sd, which gate flips add; the tie margin is not Gaussian (rms 0.05 per configuration), and the largest-lower-input composition reaches 0.01 only with oracle moments. Deduced rather than sampled, one route below the k largest lower inputs held exact cuts the tie decision's bias about 3× per k, so 1e-3 needs k = 4; adding each lower input's exact 1-D effect overstates the wedge depth by 2–16% | [margin-gaussian](angles/margin-gaussian.md), [additive-presumption](angles/additive-presumption.md), [lower_ranks](../../results/experiments/lower_ranks.txt) |
| Keep fine cells in (T, M) | With S exact and the lower inputs spread, (T, M) at their strata's medians bias the estimate by +0.019 at 9 and 36 strata per family | [sline_structure](../../results/experiments/sline_structure.txt), part 3 |
| Visit, not describe, the near-tie pieces | The error set is about 1e6 linear pieces, non-convex inside (family, record history) cells, 6–10 gate bits from any reference route; merging to 1e-3 needs over a million components; no low-dimensional surrogate holds its sign to better than 2–5 points | [exact-accuracy](angles/exact-accuracy.md), [route-mixture](angles/route-mixture.md), [low-dim-surrogates](angles/low-dim-surrogates.md) |
| Smooth the integrand across gate kinks to change the exponent | After one exact direction the integrand is Lipschitz with kinks on the gate hyperplanes, and lattice rules converge at −0.60 to −0.64, not −1; a smooth integrand in nine dimensions would need its first nine derivatives bounded | [sline_convergence](../results/experiments/sline_convergence.txt) |

## The three shapes
| Shape | What it would have to do | Why nothing tried does it |
|---|---|---|
| A rate change | Make the integrand smooth across the ~1e6 gate hyperplanes: straighten them by a change of variables, smooth the model, or enumerate the kink layer | More than ten non-parallel hyperplanes cross a cell, and Genz's sequential conditioning straightens one polytope, not a union of a hundred; a smoothed model's bias is proportional to its temperature and its derivatives to its inverse, so the constants explode; enumeration is ≥ 1e6 polytopes at ≥ 1e4 ops each, 1e10 ops to reach 1e-3 where sampling has 2e-4 |
| A low-dimensional representation of the near-tie decision, exact to 1e-3 | Name the few quantities that fix the sign of the tie margin | The decision uses all sixteen readout directions (the top-two plane agrees 37% of the time, [circuit](../../research/findings/circuit.md)); positions explain at most 26% of the margin coefficients' swing ([linearity](../../research/findings/linearity.md)); the largest lower input explains half the tie margin's variance and gate flips the rest ([tent_law](../../results/experiments/tent_law.txt)) |
| A better constant in the lattice's class | Cheaper exact nodes, allocation toward the tie strata, pairing the two families that share a tie point | Each is worth at most about 1.5×, the size of a lattice shift (the sweep's 1.3× on the held-out suite is a tie on the bench suite), and reads nothing from the weights, so it moves the baseline rather than meeting the bar |

So a mechanistic estimate that beats the baseline would have to be an account of the tie margin's sign over about a million near-tie pieces that costs less than visiting them. The account named for it, the route-dependent remainder in terms of a few route features, is measured ([lower_ladder](../../results/experiments/lower_ladder.txt), rows in [errors.md](../../research/findings/errors.md)). The coefficients' swing is the wrong target: on example/0 the S and T contributions to the tie margin each vary with variance 9,700 inside a configuration and the margin itself with 4.6, so the swing is route jumps that cancel. Of the margin's own swing inside a (family, T, M) configuration, the largest lower input's slot and value explain 42–57%, the two largest 55–71%, the lower inputs' sign count and record history under 10 points more, and the exact 160-bit route itself about 60% (a split-half lower bound). With the two largest exact and the rest Gaussian with oracle moments, P(wrong at the tie) is right to a per-configuration rms of 0.006–0.017, three to eight times better than the plain Gaussian, at 1,344 cells per configuration and oracle moments in each: thousands of passes where the lattice spends one. No presumption keyed on route features replaces the fine cells.

## Costed, not built
| Idea | Why it would help | Why it does not pay |
|---|---|---|
| A control variate: the model on the same input with the lower inputs zeroed, its mean the exact three-impulse accuracy per family | The surrogate agrees with the model on 96.5% of the example's inputs ([linearity](../../research/findings/linearity.md)), so the residual's variance is a quarter of the indicator's | The mean needs each family's correctness over the (M, S, T) simplex, about 1.2e9 ops with exact S-lines, so it can win only above 1e10 ops, where the truth cannot measure it; on the seeds the agreement is lower |
| A route tree over the lower inputs with product weights, for the seeds | Their variable gates are thresholds on the step's own input (R² 0.97), so a route's mass is nearly a product of interval probabilities and its margin is linear | The other 3% of each gate's variance shifts the thresholds with earlier inputs, an error of order 0.005 per configuration; tracking each route's linear map costs about 180 passes per configuration; the example's gates are not thresholds at all |
| Cheap S-lines where S arrives late (q ≥ 7), the sweep elsewhere | Tracking from step 8 costs 0.7 passes, not 6 | Removes about 30% more variance on 20% of roots for 0.17 passes on average: 0.73 against the sweep's 0.70 in variance × passes |
| A chain surrogate: each lower input's exact effect plus the pairwise terms of inputs a few steps apart, summed by a transfer matrix along time | The lower inputs' interactions are local: 41–50% of their variance between neighbours, 78–81% within three steps ([errors.md](../../research/findings/errors.md)) | Each pairwise term is a 2-D function, 64 passes on an 8 × 8 grid, so the six neighbouring pairs cost about 400 passes per configuration where the lattice spends one; and all 21 pairs added still overstate the wedge depth by 1–4% ([additive-presumption](angles/additive-presumption.md)) |
